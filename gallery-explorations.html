Gallery Layout — Explorations

Six ways to present the project gallery, all in the settled card language (hairline frames, mono eyebrows, serif titles) and at the live site's 800px measure. Real @Mtel projects throughout. Hover everything.

01Baseline — Feature + Grid

What's live now. One flagship at full width, the rest in a two-column grid. The reference point for everything below.

Reads: hierarchy + scannability. Risk: the grid half can feel routine if every section uses the identical pattern.
02Mosaic

Flagship as a tall plate on the left, two supporting projects stacked beside it, remainder in a tighter three-up row. Hierarchy through size, not placement — everything stays above the fold.

Reads: curated, magazine-like, dense without clutter. Risk: tall crop must flatter the flagship cover; three-up titles get small.
03Editorial Index

Ruled rows like a table of contents — serif number, title, role, framed thumbnail. The flagship row simply gets a double-size thumb. Quietest option; leans fully into the typographic voice.

Reads: confident, gallery-list elegance; scales to many projects effortlessly. Risk: covers shrink to thumbnails — weakest at selling visual work on sight.
04Zigzag Features

Every project at feature scale, covers alternating left/right. This is the "all projects get the look" direction you asked about — judge it with six real projects.

Reads: generous, each project breathes. Risk: flat hierarchy and a long corridor — six projects ≈ four viewports of scrolling; weak projects get the same stage as ThaiPass.
05Typographic Index + Hover Preview

Pure serif list — big titles, no images at rest. The cover floats in beside the cursor on hover. Maximum elegance, maximum confidence; the work reveals itself on intent.

Reads: the most sophisticated of the six — studio-grade restraint. Risk: hides the visuals by default (recruiters may never hover), and dies on touch devices — needs a mobile fallback with thumbs.
06Shelf

Horizontal scroll-snap shelf of tall 4:5 plates. Compact vertically — a whole section in one band — with momentum that invites browsing.

Reads: modern, app-like, saves vertical space across five sections. Risk: content past card three is invisible unless scrolled; horizontal scroll on desktop divides opinion.
